## Question

What is the approach to the skills community FrontierMCP depends on — the ask, the framing, and what
would be a misstep?

This is the highest-leverage venue and the only one with relationship risk, so it is decided separately
from channel sequencing. The tool exists because someone else's skills write `.scratch/`; the audience
is their users; and FrontierMCP accelerates a path those skills treat as the *fallback*, since the
recommended tracker is GitHub issues.

Decide:

- What is actually being asked for. Awareness among users, a reference from the skills' own tracker
  documentation, a contribution upstream, or nothing but a post in the community — these are different
  asks with different odds and different etiquette.
- How to frame building on someone's work without either overclaiming endorsement or appearing to fork
  their ecosystem. The divergence from the recommended tracker is real and should be stated plainly
  rather than glossed.
- Whether the approach is public (posting where users are) or direct (contacting maintainers), in what
  order, and what makes each worth someone's attention.
- What signals this went badly, and what the response is. A cold reception here does not end the effort,
  and deciding that in advance stops a bad first contact from being read as a verdict.

Assume no well-known person adopts it. The plan must work if the answer is silence.
